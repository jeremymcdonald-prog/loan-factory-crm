## Purpose

This style guide keeps Loan Factory and The Legends Mortgage Team communication clear, calm, compliant, and useful.

## Voice

- Plain English.
- Human and direct.
- Helpful without sounding desperate.
- Broker-first when options matter.
- Short paragraphs.
- One clear next step.

## Structure

1. Say why the email is being sent.
2. State the current status or request.
3. Explain the next step.
4. Add the compliance limitation if approval, pricing, closing, or funding could be misunderstood.
5. End with the sender and contact path.

## Broker Positioning

Use broker positioning when it helps the recipient understand value:

> As a broker, I can compare multiple options instead of being limited to one bank or retail lender menu.

Do not imply that broker options guarantee approval, better pricing, or faster closing.

## Do And Do Not

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Use reviewed, pending, subject to review, estimate, next step | Use guaranteed, final, best rate, definitely closing |
| Ask for secure portal upload | Ask for sensitive documents by email |
| Give Realtor-safe status | Share borrower credit, income, assets, AUS, or private conditions |
| Use short paragraphs | Over-explain when a direct next step is enough |
| Keep marketing educational | Promise results or pressure the recipient |

## Sensitive Topics

Pricing, rate locks, payments, Loan Estimates, cash to close, approval, appraisal value, title issues, insurance, closing delays, funding, and recording require careful source-backed language.

## Subject Lines

Good subject lines are specific but not private:

- `Documents needed for your loan review`
- `Closing Disclosure ready for review`
- `Quick status update`
- `Financing support for your open house`

Avoid private details in the subject line.

## Signatures

Borrower-facing and marketing-focused templates should include the approved compliance line when appropriate:

Equal Housing Opportunity. Loan Factory, Inc. NMLS 320841. Jeremy McDonald NMLS 1195266.
